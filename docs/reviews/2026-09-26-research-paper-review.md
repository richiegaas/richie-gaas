This is a strong subject for this paper: close to home, current, and with a real price at its center. The Guam Defense System is being designed right now, so the mix is still open, and a question about "where the next defense dollar should go" is exactly the kind of margin decision this course is built for. You already have a reader in mind, an appropriations staffer, and a working claim that can be wrong: "Congress should shift the margin, not the whole budget." The cost-exchange formula is clean, and your plan to show it as a range rather than a single number is the right way to handle estimates that are this uncertain. The items below are in dependency order.

**The comparison needs a common unit, and right now only interceptors have one.** Your success criterion is that a staffer can "compare the protection each option buys per dollar." The formula `C_kill = (n × C_interceptor) / P_k` gives a cost per missile defeated, but hardened shelters, dispersal and left-of-launch do not defeat missiles; they reduce what a hit destroys or raise how many missiles the attacker needs. To put all five options on one equimarginal comparison, choose a unit they can all be measured in. One that fits your framing: the number of additional missiles the attacker must buy to achieve the same damage, per defense dollar. Interceptors raise that number one way, shelters and dispersal another. If a common unit is out of reach for some option, write that in the paper and compare the rest.

**Write what would prove the working claim wrong.** The brief's "How I would know I was wrong" section is actually a success criterion, and `hypothesis:` in the front matter is still empty. Say in advance what result would sink the claim. For example: if interceptors' cost per unit of protection is already at or below the alternatives' across the cost ranges you find, the claim fails and the paper recommends buying interceptors.

**Cut the matrix to fit four pages.** Six interceptor types (SM-3 IB and IIA counted separately, SM-6, THAAD, GBI, PAC-3 MSE) × three threat types × four alternatives, plus a macro section, is more than four pages can argue. Choose the pairings that decide the question, for example the DF-26 against the interceptors assigned to it, and cruise missiles against theirs. Then compare interceptors with the one or two alternatives you can actually price. The rest can go in a sentence. One question to answer while you cut: is GBI part of Guam's defense? The descriptions of the Guam system I found name SM-3 and SM-6 on Aegis Guam and do not mention GBI. If your sources place it there, cite them; if not, drop it from the list or say why it belongs.

**In order:**

1. Choose the common unit that lets all options be compared per dollar.
2. Write the falsification condition and fill in the hypothesis.
3. Narrow to the deciding pairings and one or two alternatives, and settle whether GBI belongs.
