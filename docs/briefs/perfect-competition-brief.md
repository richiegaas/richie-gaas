# <Engagement> — engagement brief

## The problem
The problem is that I have a total of 64 beds of crops and I do not know what combination of Tomatoes, Carrots, and Mesclun I should plant and how many beds of each crop should I maintain in order to reap the most harvest, optimize efficiency, and reduce costs. I have $20,000 in fixed costs, the farmer gets paid about $50,000 per season where she spends 720 field hours and rakes in $34.72/hour, and I can have up to four temporary workers who can get paid maximum $25,000 each, billed at 1,440 hours of work time each worker, which is about a $17.36/hour wage rate. For Tomatoes, I can only have a maximum of 20 beds, and each price per bed costs $8,800, the cost of labor hours per week per bed is 2.50, the cost of fertilizer for Tomatoes is $880, and I have diminishing returns on how many Tomatoes I harvest per bed, which is 10% per bed. For Carrots, I can only have a maximum of 20 beds, and each price per bed costs $2,094, the cost of labor hours each week per bed is 0.833, the cost of fertilizer for Carrots is $440, and I have diminishing returns on how many Carrots I harvest per bed, which is 2.50% per bed. For Mesclun, I can only have a maximum of 30 beds, and each price per bed costs $2,700, the cost of labor hours each week per bed is 1.25, the cost of fertilizer for Mesclun is $880, and I have diminishing returns on how many Mesclun I harvest per bed, which is 1.25% per bed.

## What I am assuming
One assumption is that I will be able to yield all vegetables that I harvested, there will not be a bed of vegetables that spoils or that I cannot harvest. Second assumption is that pest pressure, harvest bottlenecks, and walking time do not affect the ability to reap 100% of all crops I planted in one season. Third assumption is that the farmers' market sets the prices of these crops, not me. Fourth assumption is that myself and four temporary workers are all that's needed to successfully and efficiently plant these crops and harvest. The assumptions you are taking as given, and which of them you would want
to test if you had more time.

## Hypothesis
"I expect 20 carrot beds, 30 mesclun beds, and 14 tomato beds, because this would maximize efficiency in terms of optimizing cost and wages, and because because tomatoes earn about four times per bed and I doubt the labor penalty closes that gap."

## How I would know I was wrong
I would know I was wrong if the costs of the plan from my hypothesis is more expensive than the right answer.
